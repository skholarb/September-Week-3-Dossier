Date: September 15, 2026
Entity: Trip.com Group Limited (NASDAQ: TCOM / HKEX: 9961)
Sector: Global Consumer Internet & Travel Infrastructure
Report Classification: Strategic Operational Restructuring & Risk Diagnostic
Executive Summary
Trip.com Group is executing a structural transition designed to pivot the enterprise from a traditional, volume-dependent Online Travel Agency (OTA) into a fully integrated, self-sustaining global travel operating system. Achieving this end-state requires aggressive, deliberate operational amputations. The company cannot simply acquire new assets to achieve total market dominance; it must systematically dismantle its legacy dependencies.
This diagnostic outlines the mandatory organizational sacrifices, vendor divestments, and internal restructuring Trip.com must execute across its external market posture and internal DevOps architecture to achieve terminal institutional permanence.
Phase 1: External Restructuring (Divesting Intermediaries & Enclosing Demand)
To achieve true ecosystem hegemony, Trip.com must dismantle its historical reliance on third-party gateways and transition from a fragmented multinational booking engine into a singular, closed-loop consumer environment.
1. Amputating Legacy Distribution Tolls
* The Burden: Traditional OTAs rely heavily on legacy Global Distribution Systems (GDS)—such as Amadeus, Sabre, and Travelport—for flight and hotel inventory mapping. This reliance subjects Trip.com to heavy, margin-eroding per-segment booking fees.
* The Required Restructuring: The enterprise must accelerate the construction of proprietary, direct-to-supplier infrastructure. By deploying capital to forge direct New Distribution Capability (NDC) APIs directly into airline mainframes and global hotel property management systems (PMS), the company bypasses Western intermediaries.
* Diagnostic Indicator: Success in this phase is validated by a structural compression in Cost of Revenue (CoR). If CoR inflates, it signals a failure to disintermediate legacy GDS gatekeepers.
2. The End of "Rented" Traffic
* The Burden: Trip.com, along with its acquired subsidiaries (e.g., Skyscanner), historically bleeds capital through performance marketing channels, paying heavy tolls to Google and Meta in high-stakes ad-auction bidding wars for consumer intent.
* The Required Restructuring: The company must abandon reactive, paid customer acquisition strategies. It must force total ecosystem enclosure through the aggressive deployment of its generative AI concierge, TripGenie. By utilizing conversational AI to handle end-to-end planning, booking, and in-destination services, the app transitions into an indispensable utility, capturing user intent before it ever reaches a search engine.
* Diagnostic Indicator: The ultimate proof of market enclosure is the decoupling of Sales & Marketing (S&M) expenses from revenue growth. S&M must systematically decline as organic app retention driven by AI features replaces paid traffic acquisition.
3. Transitioning to Vertical Supply Dominance
* The Burden: Operating under the illusion of "symbiotic partnerships" with global hotel chains leaves Trip.com vulnerable to direct-booking pushes from those same hospitality conglomerates.
* The Required Restructuring: Using its massive liquidity and capital reserves, Trip.com must aggressively pursue vertical integration. The firm must transition from merely distributing inventory to directly owning stakes in regional aviation assets, boutique hotel chains, and local ground-transportation infrastructure across critical Asia-Pacific and European corridors.
Phase 2: Internal DevOps & Human Capital Restructuring
The external strategy cannot succeed if the internal engineering culture remains fragmented. Trip.com must burn out organizational bloat, centralize its codebases, and remove its reliance on manual human curation.
1. Sovereign Cloud & Codebase Consolidation
* The Burden: The acquisition of foreign subsidiaries previously left the company managing siloed, market-specific tech stacks and fragmented regional databases, resulting in sluggish deployment cycles and elevated localized server costs.
* The Required Restructuring: Management must execute a ruthless internal technical audit, collapsing all international subsidiary codebases into a single, unified microservice standard. Furthermore, to combat severe cross-border data sovereignty regulations, the enterprise must heavily invest in sovereign cloud architecture—ensuring active-active global resilience that can partition European, North American, and Asian data seamlessly.
* Diagnostic Indicator: Heavy initial capital expenditure on cloud routing, followed by a stabilization of General & Administrative (G&A) compliance costs, indicates the successful deployment of a zero-trust, globally compliant data lake.
2. AI-Assisted R&D and Workforce Optimization
* The Burden: A reliance on sprawling, bloated middle-management and disconnected, isolated R&D labs that fail to rapidly deploy consumer-facing updates.
* The Required Restructuring: The enterprise must inject artificial intelligence directly into its developer sprint cycles. By fostering a "Copilot" engineering culture, human developers act as reviewers of machine-generated code, drastically increasing software release velocity. Simultaneously, the company must deploy aggressive equity compensation (RSUs) to lock its elite Natural Language Processing (NLP) and AI architects into the firm, preventing talent attrition to global tech giants.
* Diagnostic Indicator: If internal coding automation succeeds, Product Development expenses will begin to flatten relative to top-line revenue growth, proving that the company is successfully shipping complex AI features without exponentially growing engineering headcount.
3. Institutional Permanence & Central Command
* The Burden: The democratic illusion of consensus-driven board committees and an over-reliance on individual, charismatic founders or localized managing directors.
* The Required Restructuring: The company must establish absolute, centralized executive command over overarching algorithmic governance and capital deployment, while allowing local execution pods in individual markets the agility to maneuver. Ultimately, operational protocols, data-routing decisions, and risk management must be hard-coded into the company's enterprise software—ensuring the corporation functions seamlessly regardless of executive turnover.
Conclusion & Strategic Risk Horizon
Trip.com Group is executing a highly disciplined corporate transmutation. By aggressively divesting its reliance on external intermediaries—Google for traffic and Amadeus/Sabre for inventory—the company aims to permanently insulate its operating margins.
However, this transition introduces profound execution friction. As Trip.com relies more heavily on AI foundation models, it risks replacing one vendor dependency (GDS and Google) with another (LLM providers and AWS cloud compute parity). For this restructuring to be certified a success, Trip.com must prove it has not only achieved technological enclosure but also secured the proprietary, internal algorithmic sovereignty required to operate as the undisputed infrastructure of global movement.
